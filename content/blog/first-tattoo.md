Getting a tattoo had always been somewhere on my list, along with many other things I never quite got around to doing. Recently, I started to feel there was something quietly cool about taking a little control over my body, and leaving a small anchor in a time when so much is still taking shape.

The first words that came to mind were:

**Be like water.**

Water has no fixed shape. It flows, bends around things, and finds its way through without losing what it is.

I still had no idea what the tattoo should look like, so I messaged a tattoo artist on Instagram and tried to describe the feeling rather than the image: water, movement, becoming.

She somehow got it right away.

She sent me a small sketch made of a few fluid lines that could be mountains or waves.

![A minimal line drawing of overlapping mountain peaks and a winding river](/blog/first-tattoo-sketch.jpg)

No clear border, nothing too defined. It had a quiet Asian feel, like a few strokes from an ink wash painting, but there was something strong underneath them.

I knew immediately that I wanted it.

I loved the feeling of discovering something I hadn't known how to imagine myself. It felt less like I had designed a tattoo and more like I had encountered one.

And somehow, *be like water* had taken shape.

I started with a thought I couldn't quite put into words. Through a small and unexpected encounter, it became a few lines on my skin.

They don't tell me who I should become. They simply remind me that I can keep moving, keep changing, and still slowly take shape.

Like a wave.
Like a mountain.

Maybe growing up is not about finding one final shape, but becoming comfortable with taking shape as you go.
